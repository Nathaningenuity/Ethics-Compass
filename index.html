The Nine Frameworks

Methodology

Each of the 40 scenarios has a mapped-out position for all nine frameworks — what a committed utilitarian, a strict Kantian, and so on would actually say about that case. Your rating is compared against all nine at once; the closer you land to a framework's position, the higher its match score.

For instance, a utilitarian believes the organ transplant killing one to save five is the right action, since it produces the greatest net benefit — while rule utilitarians, Kantians, and libertarians all object, each for a different reason: rule utilitarians worry about what happens if this became a general practice, Kantians object to using a person merely as a means, and libertarians see it as a straightforward violation of the one person's right not to be killed. If your answer lands closest to "strongly wrong," you'll match more closely with those three; if it lands closest to "strongly right," you'll match more closely with utilitarianism.

Many scenarios come in matched pairs that hold every variable constant except one — the same trolley dilemma with a switch versus direct physical force, the bombing of civilians as an unintended side effect versus a means to achieve an end. A single answer can be explained by coincidence, but a consistent pattern across a pair — where you stay the same, or where you flip — is what actually reveals which variable your judgment is tracking.

Not every framework has a position on every scenario. Care ethics, for example, is built around real, particular relationships, so it has little to say about abstract policy questions. Where a framework has no determinate position, that scenario simply doesn't count toward its score.

This quiz is a measure of revealed moral reasoning, but it is not a measure of which theory is correct, nor a diagnosis of your character.

Ethics Instrument

The Ethics Compass

This ethics quiz uses classic ethical dilemmas and thought experiments to reveal your preferred ethical frameworks!

Every scenario isolates one variable at a time — intent versus outcome, luck versus choice, proximity versus distance, loyalty versus law, and more. Every answer is checked against nine competing ethical frameworks: utilitarianism, rule utilitarianism, Kantian deontology, virtue ethics, libertarian rights theory, Rawlsian contractualism, ethical egoism, care ethics, and desert theory.

At the end, you'll get a detailed report showing exactly which theories your instincts track most closely.
